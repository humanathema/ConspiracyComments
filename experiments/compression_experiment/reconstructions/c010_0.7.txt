A common objection to remote work is that it destroys company culture. But when you look at this claim more closely, it usually turns out to mean something narrower than it first sounds.

What it actually destroys isn't culture in general — it's the particular, largely accidental culture that grew out of one specific office's physical layout and the informal social habits that developed around it: the hallway run-ins, the shared kitchen, the seating arrangement, the after-work drinks that happened because everyone was already in the same building.

That doesn't mean no culture is possible in a remote setting. It just means that building one remotely requires deliberate, intentional effort — effort that most companies never actually planned for or budgeted, because in an office, culture seemed to happen on its own.
