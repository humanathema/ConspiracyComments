The Rosetta Stone was discovered in 1799 by French soldiers during Napoleon's military campaign in Egypt. The stone bears a single decree inscribed in three different scripts: Egyptian hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Greek.

Because the same text appeared in all three scripts, and Greek was already understood, the stone provided the key that allowed the French scholar Jean-François Champollion to decipher Egyptian hieroglyphs in 1822 — more than a thousand years after the script had fallen out of use and its meaning had been lost.
