Osmosis is the process by which water molecules move across a semi-permeable membrane, passing from an area of lower solute concentration to an area of higher solute concentration, until the concentration is equalized on both sides (or until equilibrium is reached). This movement happens passively — it's a form of diffusion driven purely by the concentration gradient, so it doesn't require any input of energy, such as ATP, to occur. This is why osmosis is often described as a passive transport process, in contrast to active transport mechanisms that do require cellular energy to move substances against a concentration gradient.
