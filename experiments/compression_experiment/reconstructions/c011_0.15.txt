The Rosetta Stone was discovered in 1799 by soldiers in Napoleon's army during his military campaign in Egypt. The stone was significant because it contained the same text inscribed in three different scripts: ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs, Egyptian Demotic script, and ancient Greek. Because scholars could already read the Greek portion, the stone provided the key needed to work out what the hieroglyphic symbols meant. It took over two decades of study before the French scholar Jean-François Champollion finally succeeded in deciphering the hieroglyphs in 1822, a breakthrough that opened up the study of ancient Egyptian civilization and language.
