What people actually fear is rarely death itself — it's the specific manner in which it might happen: the pain, the loss of control, the suffering, the drawn-out illness, the sudden violence, the being alone in the moment it happens. When we talk about "fear of death" as one big abstract thing, that abstraction actually hides what's really frightening us. It lets us avoid naming the concrete, particular thing we're actually afraid of, because the general concept of "death" is vague and almost philosophical, while the specific images — how it might happen, what it might feel like — are the things that genuinely keep us up at night.
