Suppose a train leaves City A traveling at 60 mph, and at the same time another train leaves City B, which is 300 miles away, traveling at 40 mph directly toward the first train.

Since the two trains are moving toward each other, their speeds combine: the closing speed between them is 60 + 40 = 100 mph. To find out when they meet, divide the total distance by this combined closing speed: 300 miles / 100 mph = 3 hours.

So the two trains will meet after 3 hours, regardless of which train is considered to be "traveling" and which is considered stationary — what matters is only the rate at which the distance between them is closing.
